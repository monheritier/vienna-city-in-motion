The relevant OSM-derived inputs and locally calculated route paths in this directory are offered as data under the Open Database License 1.0: https://opendatacommons.org/licenses/odbl/1-0/ . Overture transportation data also credit Overture Maps Foundation and TomTom: https://docs.overturemaps.org/attribution/ .

ODbL §4.3 requires attribution for publicly used produced works. OSMF's published PDF guidance requests the printed URL openstreetmap.org/copyright. Sections 4.4–4.6 apply share-alike and the offer of the derivative database or permitted alternatives when their stated conditions are met; §4.7 addresses technical restrictions. A PDF produced work is not itself automatically required to be licensed ODbL. Whether a particular extract is substantial or a derivative database is fact-specific.

Included: osm_road_graph_source.gpkg (40,828 features); the concept_corridors.geojson output; wien_station_records.gpkg and ../scripts/reproduce_routes.py document the non-OSM anchors and route algorithm. OSM map extracts include osm_water_for_maps.gpkg, osm_rail_for_maps.gpkg, overture_roads_for_maps.gpkg and osm_buildings_part_1.gpkg and osm_buildings_part_2.gpkg. These were filtered, clipped and/or reprojected; the building parts divide the source by fid. The route graph is exploratory, not a validated pedestrian graph.

At the 22 November 2026 audit, these files were local and not yet a publicly accessible offer. If the repository is published, verify that the data can be obtained without restriction and provide its actual URL when sharing the PDF; preserve access under the applicable terms. Check all map inputs and additional contents used in each derived database; inclusion of some extracts alone does not establish complete compliance.

The Microsoft Global ML Building Footprints upstream repository currently states CDLA Permissive 2.0 at https://github.com/microsoft/GlobalMLBuildingFootprints/blob/main/LICENSE ; Overture's current attribution page identifies its Buildings theme as ODbL and attributes Microsoft. Apply terms to the exact source and release actually used; figure-by-figure Microsoft provenance remains unresolved here.
